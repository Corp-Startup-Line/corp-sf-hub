3 Objection Handling
1 "We already have insurance"

90% of the companies we work with were already covered — they just didn't know they were paying a 25–35% broker markup on top of what they actually needed. By law, you can cancel your current policy anytime and get a prorated refund. Ten minutes shows you whether there's a gap. Worth a look?

If they say their rate is fine

Totally possible — and if the numbers back that up, I'll tell you. But most founders are surprised. That's the whole point of the review: it's free and it's fast.

2 "We're too early / too small"

Most founders say that — until a customer contract or an investor diligence checklist asks for proof of coverage and they need it yesterday. The earlier you sort it, the less it costs and the less it distracts you later. Even if you're not buying today, a 10-minute review tells you exactly when you'll need what, so you're not scrambling.

If they push back again

What does your customer contract situation look like right now — are you signing agreements with clients?

3 "Not interested"

Totally fair. One quick thing before I let you go — do you have customer contracts or investor agreements in place right now?

If yes

Then you're probably already exposed. Takes 10 minutes to know for sure — and it's free. That's the only ask.

If no / still not interested

Understood. Can I follow up in a month when things might have moved? I'll put a note in and reach out then.

4 "Just send me an email"

Usually my customers have more questions once I send an email. How about this — I send you an email and a tentative invite for next week. If you like what we do, we confirm the time. If you don't, you cancel it. Fair?

1 How Startups Actually Buy
The 3 forcing functions

Startups don't buy insurance because they want to — they buy because something forces the decision. If one of these exists, insurance will come up.

CustomersProcurement, MSAs, COIs, security reviews
Investors & boardsGovernance, diligence, D&O requirements
Landlords & vendorsLeases, events, vendor onboarding
Quick picks · match the trigger
  • Selling B2B / enterprise contracts→Tech E&O, Cyber, CGL
  • Fundraising / board involvement→D&O
  • Hiring or managing people→EPLI
  • Handling customer data or payments→Cyber
  • Leases, vendors, events→CGL
2 Policy Battle Cards
D&O
Founder & Board ProtectionDirectors & Officers
Often required by VCs
CoversThe personal assets of founders, executives, and board members.
Why it mattersLeadership decisions get challenged by investors, employees, or advisors. These claims target people, not just the company.
Shows upFundraising · board formation · investor disputes
TriggerTerm sheet, priced round, or adding independent directors
"Protects you personally if an investor, employee, or board member challenges leadership decisions."
Tech E&O
Product LiabilityErrors & Omissions
CoversClaims that your product, software, or AI didn't perform as expected and caused customer financial loss.
Why it mattersCustomers don't sue because of bugs — they sue because bugs cost them money.
Shows upPaying customers · enterprise contracts · SLAs or implementation work
TriggerMSA/SOW insurance requirements or procurement review
"If a customer says your product cost them money, this is the policy that responds."
Cyber
Data & Breach RiskCyber Liability
CoversCosts from data exposure, hacks, ransomware, and system breaches.
Why it mattersStartups move fast, store sensitive data, and rely on modern infrastructure — breaches happen early and often.
Shows upHandling customer data · APIs and integrations · security questionnaires
TriggerCustomer security review or handling regulated/sensitive data
"If data gets exposed or systems get locked, this is what pays to respond."
CGL
Basic Business RiskCommercial General Liability
Often contractually required
CoversEveryday accidents involving bodily injury or property damage tied to the business.
Why it mattersBaseline coverage most third parties expect before working with you.
Shows upOffice leases · vendors and partners · events and onsite work
TriggerLease signature, vendor onboarding, or event venue requirements
"Baseline coverage landlords and partners expect before doing business."
EPLI
Employee LawsuitsEmployment Practices Liability
Critical as hiring begins
CoversClaims from employees or candidates — discrimination, harassment, retaliation, wrongful termination.
Why it mattersPeople-related risk grows faster than founders expect.
Shows upFirst hires · manager promotions · performance issues or terminations
TriggerHiring ramp or first termination
"Once you hire, this protects you if someone challenges how they were treated."
Media
Marketing & Content RiskMedia Liability
CoversClaims tied to ads, websites, and content — copyright, defamation, misrepresentation.
Why it mattersMarketing scales faster than legal review.
Shows upPaid ads · publishing content · competitive categories or AI-generated content
TriggerScaling marketing, PR, or content-heavy features
"Protects you if your marketing or content triggers a legal claim."
HNOA
Hired & Non-Owned Auto
CoversCompany liability when employees drive personal or rented vehicles for work.
Why it mattersEven if you don't own cars, your company can still be named in an auto-related claim.
Shows upSales teams · business travel · enterprise contract checklists
TriggerCustomer contract requires auto liability
"Covers the company if an employee causes an accident while driving for work."
Fiduciary
Benefits & 401(k) Risk
CoversClaims tied to managing employee benefit plans like 401(k)s and health plans.
Why it mattersBenefits decisions create fiduciary responsibility — even when vendors are outsourced.
Shows upLaunching a 401(k) · scaling benefits · later-stage diligence
TriggerOffering a 401(k) or expanding benefits
"Protects leadership if employees challenge how benefits are managed."
3 Common Startup Mistakes
  • Waiting until a contract or term sheet is due
  • Assuming CGL covers product failures
  • Skipping D&O until investors demand it
  • Underinsuring cyber despite real data exposure
  • Forgetting HNOA because the company doesn't own vehicles

Direct: D&O, Cyber, Tech E&O, EPLI. Anything else, we place it.

1 Rules of the Room
  • Consequence first. Lead with the number they're losing, not a pleasantry. "Came in at roughly $8k last time. Honestly think there's a better number here."
  • Short declarative lines. One idea per line. No paragraphs, no "would love to," no filler.
  • Always name a dollar figure. A real stat beats an adjective. "Pulled $19k back for a founder last week on D&O and Cyber alone."
  • One ask per send. A time, a doc, or an accept. Never two.
  • Same-day close. "If you like the number, you're covered that day — we handle cancelling your existing policies."
  • No em-dashes in body copy. Use periods. Keep it clean.
2 Cold Open · No Prior Quote
Locked SOP "your broker is keeping something from you"
Subject: your broker is keeping something from you
Hey [First], Most founders overpay 20-50% on business insurance. Not because they have to. Because a broker sits in the middle and takes a cut. We're Corgi (YC S24). An actual carrier built for startups. No broker, no markup. You talk straight to the people who set the price. 15 minutes shows you what you're paying vs what you should be. Number's yours either way. Worth a look? Talk soon, [Your Name] P.S. - Shot you a request on LinkedIn
Locked body. Subject and P.S. are fixed. This is the cold-email SOP - don't rewrite the core lines. For the LinkedIn follow-up variant, open with "Good to connect." and reference the connection in the P.S.
Short variant "actual insurance carrier"
Subject: [Company] x Corgi
Hey [First], Tried pinging you earlier... We're Corgi (YC S24), an actual insurance carrier built for startups. Most companies overpay 20-50% by using brokers instead of going directly to a carrier like us. Worth a free 15-min call to see what you're paying vs what you should be? Talk soon, [Your Name]
When: a quick, punchy first touch on a fresh prospect - especially paired with a call attempt ("tried pinging you earlier"). Shorter than the locked SOP.
3 Reactivation · Dormant Quote
Touch 1 "Been a while.."
Subject: Been a while..  /  Still a priority?  /  Still looking to get covered?
[First] - Still have your quote for [Company]'s policies for Tech, Cyber etc.. Came in at roughly $[X]k last time. Honestly think there's a better number here. 15 mins with our underwriter, and we'll know for sure. Have priorities shifted, or is this still something you're looking to finalize? Talk soon, [Your Name]
When: a quote that went quiet. Swap the coverage lines to match what they were quoted. The dollar figure is the hook — always include it.
4 The Bump Sequence
Touch 2 The buried-email bump
Reply on the same thread. Keep the original subject.
Bumping this in case it got buried.. Pulled $19k back for a founder last week on D&O and Cyber alone. That money didn't disappear. His broker was just keeping it. Talk soon, [Your Name]
When: ~24h after Touch 1 with no reply. Social proof + the broker-margin stat does the work.
Touch 3 "3rd time's the charm"
Same thread. Final bump before you rest the lead.
Hopefully the 3rd time's the charm here. I get it... insurance is slow and boring. That's not us. We cut the broker out - you talk straight to the carrier. If you like the number, you're covered that day. Worth 15 minutes? Talk soon, [Your Name]
When: ~24h after Touch 2. If this one dies, note it in HubSpot and set a reactivation date. Don't keep hammering.
5 Booked-Call Confirmation
On booking "Here's what we're doing in 15 minutes"
Reply on thread the moment they pick a time.
[First] - [Day] works! CCing [Underwriter], our Lead Underwriter - he'll be running the numbers live on the call. Hit accept if you have a sec. Here's what we're doing in 15 minutes: → Underwrite [Company] in real time, in front of you → Show you exactly what you're exposed to and what it costs → Leave you with a number - no follow-up needed If you like the quote, you're covered on the same day, and we handle cancelling your existing policies. P.S. - Customer stories here if you want them: corgi.insure/customers Talk soon, [Your Name]
Always: CC the underwriter, send the calendar accept, and BCC HubSpot. The three bullets never change.
